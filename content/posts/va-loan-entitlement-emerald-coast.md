**Yes, you can use your VA loan again. Most veterans and service members can buy another home with a VA-backed loan by restoring entitlement after selling and paying off the old loan, or by using remaining entitlement while keeping the first home. On the Emerald Coast, that matters for families who PCS in, move up, or retire near Eglin AFB and Hurlburt Field.**

We're Angee Kent, REALTOR® with LPT Realty, and Jay Spurlin (NMLS 183681), mortgage loan originator with Coastal Funding Corporation (NMLS 103035). Veterans Day is Wednesday, November 11, and it's a good moment to explain one of the most misunderstood VA benefits: it isn't one-and-done.

## Key Takeaways

- **The VA home loan benefit is reusable.** You can restore entitlement or use what's left.
- **Full entitlement means no VA loan limit.** Your lender still has to approve the amount you can afford.
- **Keeping your first home?** Your remaining entitlement is based on the county conforming limit, $832,750 for a one-unit home in 2026 in Escambia, Santa Rosa, and Okaloosa Counties.
- **Funding fees are higher after first use** with less than 5% down: 3.3% versus 2.15%, per the VA.
- **Many veterans pay no funding fee at all,** including those receiving VA disability compensation.

## What Is VA Loan Entitlement?

**Entitlement** is the amount the VA guarantees to repay your lender if the loan goes into default. It's not cash, and it's not the most you can borrow. The VA explains that basic entitlement is $36,000. For loans over $144,000, the VA guarantees up to 25% of the loan amount through what it calls bonus entitlement.

Your **Certificate of Eligibility (COE)** shows your entitlement. If your COE says your basic entitlement is $36,000, you have **full entitlement**. With full entitlement, the VA says there is no loan limit, as long as you qualify and the appraisal supports the price.

## Can You Restore VA Entitlement?

Yes. According to [VA.gov's eligibility page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/eligibility/), you may be able to restore entitlement you used in the past if at least one of these is true:

1. **You sold the home** you bought with the prior VA loan and paid that loan in full.
2. **A qualified veteran assumes your loan** and substitutes their own entitlement.
3. **You paid the prior loan in full but kept the home.** The VA allows this one-time restoration only once.

You can request a COE with restoration online, ask your lender to request it for you, or file VA Form 26-1880.

## Can You Have Two VA Loans at Once?

Often, yes. This is common for military families who keep their first home as a rental after PCS orders. If you don't sell, part of your entitlement stays tied to the first loan, and you use your **remaining entitlement** for the next home.

Here's a simplified example. It is an illustration only, not a quote or approval:

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| 2026 one-unit conforming limit in Okaloosa County | $832,750 |
| 25% of that limit | $208,187.50 |
| Entitlement still used on your first VA loan | $75,000 (example) |
| Remaining entitlement | $133,187.50 |
| Approximate maximum loan with $0 down (4 x remaining) | $532,750 |

If the price is above that amount, you can still use your VA loan. A lender would typically ask for a down payment to cover the gap. Your exact numbers depend on your COE, and Jay can review it with you.

## What Does the VA Funding Fee Cost After First Use?

The [VA funding fee](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/funding-fee-and-closing-costs/) is a one-time charge that can usually be paid at closing or financed. For purchase loans, the VA lists:

| Down payment | First use | After first use |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 5% | 2.15% | 3.3% |
| 5% or more | 1.5% | 1.5% |
| 10% or more | 1.25% | 1.25% |

On a $450,000 purchase with no down payment, that's a $9,675 fee on first use versus $14,850 on a later use. Putting 5% down drops the fee to 1.5% either way.

**Who pays no funding fee?** The VA says you're exempt if you receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability, are eligible for it but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead, receive Dependency and Indemnity Compensation as a surviving spouse, have a qualifying pre-discharge rating, or are an active-duty member who received a Purple Heart.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use my VA loan more than once?

Yes. The VA home loan benefit can be reused. You can restore entitlement after selling and paying off a prior VA loan, or use remaining entitlement to buy again while you keep the first home.

### How do I restore my VA loan entitlement?

Sell the home and pay the prior loan in full, have a qualified veteran assume the loan with their entitlement, or pay the loan in full and keep the home, which the VA allows only once. Then request a COE with restoration.

### Can I have two VA loans at the same time?

Often, yes. If part of your entitlement is still tied to your first loan, you can use your remaining entitlement. In higher-priced cases, a down payment may be needed to cover the gap.

### What is the VA funding fee for a second VA loan?

For a purchase after first use, the VA lists 3.3% with less than 5% down, 1.5% with 5% or more down, and 1.25% with 10% or more down. Some veterans, such as those receiving VA disability compensation, are exempt.

### Is there a VA loan limit in 2026?

Not if you have full entitlement. If some entitlement is still in use, your zero-down amount is based on the county conforming limit, which is $832,750 for a one-unit home in Escambia, Santa Rosa, and Okaloosa Counties in 2026.
