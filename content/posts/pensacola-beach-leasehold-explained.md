**When you buy a home on Pensacola Beach, you buy a leasehold, not the land.** The land belongs to **Escambia County** and is leased through the **Santa Rosa Island Authority (SRIA)**, usually in terms of up to 99 years. You own the home or condo and the remaining lease term, and you can usually finance, sell, and pass it on much like any other home.

We're **Angee Kent (LPT Realty)** and **Jay Spurlin (Coastal Funding Corporation, NMLS 183681)**. Leaseholds confuse a lot of buyers, so here's our plain-English guide from both the real estate side and the mortgage side.

## Key takeaways

- **The land is county-owned** and leased through the SRIA, which was created in 1947.
- **Leases run up to 99 years.** Check how many years remain and whether the lease is renewable.
- **Financing is available**, but lenders review the lease. Fannie Mae requires at least five years remaining beyond the loan's maturity.
- **No fee-simple conversion program exists** as of October 2026.
- **After Hurricane Isaias,** the county estimated about $11.5 million in residential damage on the island.

## What is a leasehold, and why is Pensacola Beach different?

**Leasehold (definition):** the right to use land for a set term under a lease, plus ownership of the buildings on it. **Fee simple** is the full ownership most homes have, with land and building together.

According to the [Santa Rosa Island Authority](https://www.sria-fla.com/about-santa-rosa-island-authority-sria):

- The Florida Legislature created the SRIA in **1947** after the federal government conveyed Santa Rosa Island to Escambia County.
- Because of restrictions in that federal conveyance, **land on Pensacola Beach may not be purchased.** Instead, the SRIA leases it for terms of up to 99 years.
- Pensacola Beach covers about **1,474 acres** along roughly an **eight-mile** stretch of the island. About **60%** is public use or public service land, and 40% is leased for homes and businesses.
- The SRIA doesn't receive tax revenue. It's funded by lease fees.

That's a big reason Pensacola Beach still has so much open, public beach.

## How do lease fees and property taxes work?

Expect two kinds of costs beyond your mortgage:

1. **Lease fees** paid to the SRIA. Amounts depend on the lease.
2. **Property taxes**, depending on the lease type. The Pensacola News Journal has reported that Florida courts found leases that renew automatically are essentially ownership and can be taxed, while leases that must be renegotiated may be treated differently.

**Our tip:** before you make an offer, ask for the **lease, its renewal terms, the last few years of lease fee and tax bills, and HOA documents** for condos. Your title company and the Escambia County Property Appraiser's records help confirm the details.

## Can you get a mortgage on a Pensacola Beach leasehold?

**Yes.** Leasehold homes and condos on Pensacola Beach are financed regularly. The difference is that the lender reviews the lease as well as the property.

- **Remaining term:** Fannie Mae's [Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-3-03/special-property-eligibility-and-underwriting-considerations-leasehold-estates) requires the lease's unexpired term to run **at least five years beyond the loan's maturity date.** For a 30-year loan, that means at least 35 years left on the lease.
- **Lease provisions:** lenders look for terms that protect the mortgage, such as notice of default and the ability to transfer the lease.
- **Condos:** the building's project approval, insurance, and reserves matter too.
- **Loan sizes:** the 2026 conforming limit is **$832,750**. Many beach homes exceed it, which means a jumbo loan. In September 2026, Pensacola MLS data showed a **$772,500** median sale price in ZIP 32561, which includes Pensacola Beach (Beyond Pensacola).
- **Second homes and rentals:** the island is popular for getaways and vacation rentals. Coastal Funding offers **DSCR loans** for investment properties that qualify based on rental income.

**Lender-side tip:** order the lease review early. When leasehold questions show up late, closings get delayed.

## Is fee simple conversion coming?

Not right now. Because the restrictions come from the 1946 federal law behind the 1947 conveyance, **only Congress can lift them.** A House bill passed in 2014 but did not become law, and the Pensacola News Journal reported a later effort failed in the Senate. As of October 2026, there's **no conversion program** for Pensacola Beach leaseholders. Be careful with online claims otherwise; they often refer to Okaloosa County's separate program.

The SRIA board has also discussed how often leases can be renewed, according to Island Times, so ask about the board's current policy.

## Pensacola Beach after Hurricane Isaias

WEAR News reported on October 20, 2026, that Escambia County estimated **$11.5 million in residential damage** on the island, with homes on the east end hit hardest. SRIA officials credited a **$35 million beach renourishment project** completed earlier in 2026 with protecting infrastructure, and the beach avoided major storm surge. The beach is open, Fort Pickens is open for daily use, and the **toll to get onto Pensacola Beach is suspended for 60 days** starting October 8.

**Realtor-side tip:** for any beach property right now, get a fresh inspection, ask for storm repair records, confirm insurance before your inspection period ends, and check the home's FEMA flood zone.

## A little island history

- **Fort Pickens**, on the western tip of Santa Rosa Island, was completed in the 1830s and stayed in Union hands throughout the Civil War. Today it's part of **Gulf Islands National Seashore**.
- **Navarre Beach**, at the other end of the island, is also county-owned land under a similar arrangement. See our [Navarre FL homes guide](../navarre-fl-homes-buyer-guide/).
